Cheon and Hwang conjectured in 1992 that E_k(r) + E_k(c) - P_k(A) <= 2 - k!/n^k
for every non-negative n x n matrix of total sum n and every 1 <= k <= n, with
equality only at J_n/n; the endpoint k = n is Dittert's conjecture. We prove
the case k = 3 for every n >= 4 -- the inequality, the equality case, and a
quantitative strengthening of both -- by a single Positivstellensatz
certificate whose nineteen symmetry-reduced coefficients are explicit rational
functions of n. The strengthening is

    (2 - 6/n^3) - [E_3(r) + E_3(c) - P_3(A)]  >=  theta_2(n) * ||A - J_n/n||_F^2

with theta_2(n) = (n^4 + 40 n^2 - 84 n + 40) / (n^5 (n-1)^3 (n-2)), which
contains the equality case as the special case where the left side vanishes.
Together with Hwang's 1987 theorem at n = 3 this settles k = 3 in every
dimension. The proof is uniform in n because the governing degree is k, not n:
the symmetry-reduced program has the same 12 x 19 shape at every dimension, so
its solution is computed once over Q(n). Positive definiteness of the two
n^2 x n^2 Gram matrices is reduced in closed form -- Bose-Mesner algebra of the
rook's graph for one, the representation theory of S_(n-1) for the other -- to
the positivity of ten rational functions of n (nine independent; the tenth
equals one of the other nine divided by n), each decided on n >= 4 by a Sturm
sequence over Q.

The k = 3 certificate sits inside an exact decomposition of the deficit that is
uniform in n and in k. The case k = 2, which is classical, follows from that
decomposition in a few lines; we give the derivation and a machine-checked
proof. We also formalise, for every 2 <= k <= n, the confinement of any
violator to a shrinking neighbourhood of the Birkhoff polytope -- at k = n this
is Theorem 2.1 of Cheon and Wanless 2012, and the extension to all k is a
routine transfer of their method -- together with the Newton and Maclaurin
inequalities that the confinement consumes, neither of which is in Mathlib.
Every result stated below as a theorem is proved in Lean 4 with no sorry and on
Lean's standard axioms, and is cited by its declaration name.
